## Renderer decision

CesiumJS 1.145.0 renders at device resolution (`useBrowserRecommendedResolution: false`). `resolutionScale` is 1.25 when device pixel ratio is below 1.5 and 1 otherwise. The tested browser reported DPR 1.1 and a 1600 × 900 drawing buffer in a 1280 × 720 viewport. The scene requests 4× MSAA; FXAA is disabled where WebGL2 MSAA is available and enabled as a fallback where it is not. HDR remains off to retain the established material response; the public HDR control was inspected, but no evidence justified a tone-curve change in this pass. The native scene Sun, sun bloom and a restrained `glowFactor` of 0.6 retain Cesium's scene-time direction. The same clock lights the globe and moves the terminator.

An 8× MSAA trial reported roughly 55 FPS but rendered the globe and skybox black in the tested in-app browser, so 4× is the highest **visually stable** tested setting. At 4×, Earth Overview measured about 43–50 FPS and close ISS Inspection about 52–54 FPS in repeated five-second windows at 1600 × 900. These are local browser observations, not cross-device guarantees. The inspected development and production tabs reported no WebGL error or GPU-memory warning at the selected 4× setting; GPU memory use itself was not instrumented. `Scene.maximumCubeMapSize` reported 16,384; that limit alone does not make a 4,096-face skybox worthwhile. Six 2,048² RGB faces require roughly 72 MiB uncompressed before cube mip levels; 4,096² would require roughly 288 MiB. The latter adds cost for little apparent benefit in this viewport. Lower-power hardware may render more slowly; the viewer checks GPU MSAA support and skybox cube-size support.

A later served-production regression ran at DPR 2 with a 2560 × 1440 drawing buffer. Five-second diagnostic windows reported roughly 22–28 FPS in Overview, 25–27 FPS during approach, and 25–27 FPS in Inspection. These lower figures reflect the larger rendering buffer and this particular browser session; they are not a hardware-wide benchmark. No GPU-memory value was measured.

Cesium's `ImageryLayer` uses the highest supported anisotropy when `maximumAnisotropy` is omitted. The tiled Earth textures use Cesium's ordinary bilinear/mipmap filtering. The station's glTF sampler explicitly requests linear magnification and trilinear minification (`magFilter=9729`, `minFilter=9987`), which causes mipmaps to be used/generated by the runtime. Its power-of-two PNG images are lossless; the GLB does not embed a precomputed mip chain. Cesium's public `Model` API does not expose a per-texture anisotropy setting, so no unsupported private WebGL modification is made.

## Imagery and sky decisions

The production day and night strategy is **`NASA_GIBS_TILED`**. NASA's current [EPSG:3857 WMTS capabilities](https://gibs.earthdata.nasa.gov/wmts/epsg3857/best/1.0.0/WMTSCapabilities.xml) advertise static `BlueMarble_NextGeneration` JPEG and dated `VIIRS_Black_Marble` PNG in `GoogleMapsCompatible_Level8`. MISSION1 caps both at level 8. The day mosaic depicts historical August 2004 Blue Marble imagery; the night layer requests the fixed **2016-01-01** Black Marble composite and uses `dayAlpha=0`, `nightAlpha=1`. At the equator, level 8's 256-pixel tiles provide about 600 m per tile pixel, versus roughly 11 km per pixel in the former 3600 × 1800 local Black Marble image. Actual source detail and latitude vary; neither product is current weather or live lighting. A live tile request was HTTP 200 with permissive browser CORS. A real image probe runs before each network layer is added. Bundled Natural Earth II remains under the day layer; the previous local Black Marble JPEG is the night fallback. On a provider error, the affected GIBS layer is removed so the local image shows through. No Cesium ion token is required.

A local high-resolution tiled derivative was evaluated. It would remove the NASA network dependency but require downloading and distributing a global high-resolution tile pyramid. The existing small local maps already cover outages; NASA GIBS supplies zoom-dependent high detail without that repository and deployment cost. This is a frontend rendering choice and does not add a backend data source or service.

The sky is generated from [NASA Goddard SVS Tycho Catalog Sky Map](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/3572), a 16,384 × 8,192 celestial plate-carrée JPEG. The downloaded source is 25,039,837 bytes, SHA-256 `1e08506a6b18d151be51964f424a7327465eea64887eff7080753aadeff0a1ba`. Run `python3 frontend/scripts/generate-tycho-skybox.py SOURCE.jpg frontend/public/skybox 2048 frontend/src/cesium/skyboxManifest.json` from the repository root, with Python, Pillow 12.3.0 and NumPy 2.3.5. The script maps right ascension and declination to Cesium's TEME-oriented cube directions (+X RA 0°, +Y RA 90°, +Z north celestial pole), samples the full-resolution source bilinearly, and compares all 12 pairs of cube-face edge pixels. The check passed 12/12. The six lossless PNGs total 17,421,296 bytes; their individual SHA-256, byte counts and 2048² dimensions are pinned in `skyboxManifest.json` and verified by tests. The default Cesium stars remain until every custom face decodes, and remain if a face fails or the GPU cube limit is below 2048. A persistent Cesium credit links to the NASA GSFC SVS map when the custom skybox is active. Stars are a visual celestial backdrop; no sky-map epoch is used as a spacecraft attitude or timing source.

For the final integrity check, the NASA source was downloaded again and matched the pinned hash and byte count. Running the generator into a temporary directory reproduced the manifest and all six checked-in PNGs byte for byte; all 12 shared edge pairs matched. A temporary served-production test made the `negativeZ` face unavailable. The custom-cube completion event did not fire and the default star sky remained visible, without a partial cube or black face. The failure override was removed from the final source and build.

**Sun decision: `NATIVE_CESIUM_SUN`.** Cesium's public Sun visual controls preserve the scene-time celestial direction with a restrained native glow at the selected device resolution. A static NASA/SDO surface image would not depict the current solar surface and would require a separately validated ephemeris-aligned replacement; the native Sun is the more truthful and stable result here. No second Sun or fabricated solar position is introduced. The test camera did not bring the solar disc into a comparable close frame, so near-Sun appearance remains a visual QA limit rather than a claimed improvement.

## Station texture decision

The [NASA VTAD source GLB](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/international-space-station-3d-model/) is 44,495,916 bytes, SHA-256 `26dba905b4b7555edbcb0c5f5a61b5c18659f5166076ab27dbb0e64025759fca`. It has 26 embedded PNG entries, 19 unique images after deduplication, all 512² or 1024². Four material uses include normal maps. Alpha channels are opaque. The previous 15,713,052-byte semantic WebP GLB kept the same pixel dimensions but was lossy (quality 90); per-image RGB PSNR against NASA source measured about 40.6–47.2 dB. A high-fidelity KTX2/UASTC path was evaluated, but the tested toolchain lacks KTX-Software's `toktx` encoder and no superior stable browser result was established. The selected lossless PNG derivative preserves the exact NASA image bytes and original PBR materials instead of introducing another lossy transform. Source texture resolution remains limited to 1024²; no invented detail is claimed.

Reproduce the source-quality semantic derivative with glTF Transform CLI 4.5.1: `gltf-transform dedup ISS_stationary.glb iss-dedup-png.glb`, then `python3 frontend/scripts/add-verified-module-features.py iss-dedup-png.glb frontend/public/models/iss-vtad-source-png.glb`. The deduplicated base is 32,197,496 bytes, SHA-256 `ce2548b56741ee764de7e1d593f1c50785a54150ebc500ed5424a4f5bdf8f657`. The final 32,735,516-byte GLB is SHA-256 `ce23515c6bf4a77d3bac6b7c2554e3c92e137c11c8db3ee1ffc1f3ef2030a16f`. The script verifies the base hash, adds exactly the 14 previously verified feature identities, and changes only glTF sampler metadata beyond the semantic additions. Validation found zero structural errors; known source tangent/degenerate-geometry warnings remain. No mesh simplification or material relighting was added. The source's white modules, metallic structures, radiators and arrays retain their authored PBR response. Night-side visibility is therefore governed by Cesium scene lighting, with no invented station lights.

`Model.fromGltfAsync` begins at Viewer startup with `incrementallyLoadTextures: false` and `show: false`. The ready event, after texture and draw resources are ready, also checks all 14 named semantic nodes before marking the model ready. The marker remains until then. One model primitive persists through Overview, approach, Inspection and return; view changes only affect camera and visibility. The existing 1,500/2,000 km locator hysteresis avoids rapid marker changes near a threshold. In warm development-browser trials, PNG model readiness took roughly 0.5–0.8 seconds; the first served-production load in the test browser took 3.7 seconds while the marker remained visible. The larger GLB increases disk/network transfer compared with WebP but prevents progressive low-resolution texture replacement. No white or missing-texture station frame was observed in the completed approach and inspection trials; tests still need to be repeated on other GPUs and cold networks. Source geometry's known degenerate triangles remain a possible source-limited depth artifact, separate from texture streaming.

The final served-production regression also used a cache-busting GLB URL on the local preview server. Texture and feature readiness took about **1.1 seconds** in that run; an earlier fresh production tab took **5.9 seconds**. These are local observations and do not model a slow network. The marker preceded the ready model; no white, untextured or missing-texture frame, model disappearance, or blank marker-to-model interval was observed. A temporary camera-distance control crossed 1,490/1,550 km and 1,900/2,010 km twice: the far locator changed only below 1,500 km or above 2,000 km, while the model remained ready and visible and the primitive count stayed at three. At least three complete Overview → approach → Inspection → Overview cycles retained one model-ready event, one skybox decode and one GIBS probe pair; no remount, texture reload flash, skybox flash, blank Earth frame or camera-state degradation was observed. The temporary test controls were removed before the final build. The 32,735,516-byte PNG GLB is **17,022,464 bytes larger** than the previous WebP derivative, and the six skybox PNGs add **17,421,296 bytes** of runtime assets.

The corrected moving-target entry raises the model's presentation-only minimum footprint smoothly from 72 to 150 pixels over five seconds, then retains 150 in Inspection. During the 1.2-second return flight it eases back to 72, avoiding an immediate size pop when Back is pressed. The GLB's physical scale, maximumScale 20000, texture readiness, semantic features and persistent primitive are unchanged. A development-only pivot point and screen-space probe confirmed the asset bounding-sphere center coincides with the sampled ISS point (rounded 0 m). The display curve helps the station grow throughout approach; it does not alter orbit coordinates or suggest physical scale.

Use `?graphicsDiagnostics` on the development URL to log supported MSAA/cube size, rendering resolution, real GIBS probe outcomes, sky decode time, model readiness time and five-second frame-rate windows. This is development-only observation code; it does not change ISS data semantics.
